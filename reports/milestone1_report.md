# Milestone 1: data pipeline report (MILK10k)

Sebastián Ríos Saucedo · EADA · Computer Vision & Speech Recognition · splits created 2026-10-03, seed 42

## 1. Label strategy
**Primary target: `diagnosis_1`, keeping Indeterminate as a 3rd class.** At lesion level the data contain 3,634 Malignant (69.4%), 1,483 Benign (28.3%) and 123 Indeterminate (2.3%) lesions. All Indeterminate lesions are AKIEC (actinic keratoses, "indeterminate epidermal proliferations"), while the other 180 AKIEC lesions are Malignant (Bowen's disease / SCC in situ). *Dropping* Indeterminate would remove exactly the pre-malignant lesions a triage tool must not ignore. *Merging* it into Benign would teach the model that an actinic keratosis needs no action, and merging into Malignant would mix two different clinical pathways (treat/monitor vs. excise). With a weighted loss, 88 training lesions are enough to keep it as its own class. For triage, Indeterminate + Malignant are also reported together as "needs work-up".

**Stretch target: 11 → 8 classes.** DF (52), INF (50), VASC (47), BEN_OTH (44) and MAL_OTH (9 lesions) are rare. Keeping them all with class weights gives weights up to about 50x, so the gradient is dominated by a handful of images (overfitting, unstable training), and MAL_OTH has 1-2 lesions in val/test, which cannot be evaluated. Merging everything into one "other" class would mix benign and malignant lesions, which is clinically unacceptable. Compromise: the four rare **benign** classes become `OTHER_BENIGN` (193 lesions), and `MAL_OTH` is kept separate as `OTHER_MALIGNANT` with a capped weight (20) and reported with raw counts. Splits remain stratified on the original 11 classes, so the decision can be revisited later. The mapping is saved in `outputs/label_map.json`. Note that `dx → diagnosis_1` is not a function (AKIEC), so diagnosis_1 is predicted directly, not derived.

## 2. Split design and verification
Lesions are split first, and images follow their lesion. `split_lesions()` uses StratifiedGroupKFold (groups = lesion_id) twice: one fold as test, then one fold of the remainder as val. Stratification key = 11-class `dx` refined by `diagnosis_1` (AKIEC|Indeterminate vs AKIEC|Malignant), so the small Indeterminate class is also balanced. Result: **3,742 / 749 / 749 lesions** (71.4 / 14.3 / 14.3%; 7,484 / 1,498 / 1,498 images). Verified in code: zero lesion overlap for every pair of splits; every lesion has exactly 2 images (one per type) in its split; maximum deviation of any 11-class proportion from the global one is **0.09 pp**; diagnosis_1 proportions are Benign 28.3/28.3/28.4, Indeterminate 2.35/2.40/2.27, Malignant 69.4/69.3/69.3 (%, train/val/test). A property test over 10 seeds checks overlap, sizes (±1 pp) and reproducibility. Every rare class appears in val and test for 10/10 seeds, but MAL_OTH has only 1-2 lesions there.

**Imbalance on TRAIN (lesions):** Malignant 2,596 : Benign 1,058 : Indeterminate 88, so the largest/smallest ratio is **29.5 : 1**. For 11 classes BCC 1,802 : MAL_OTH 7 = **257 : 1** (8 classes: same ratio, OTHER_BENIGN = 137). Handling, both computed from the train split only: class weights `N/(K·n_c)` for the loss (`outputs/class_weights.json`), and a `WeightedRandomSampler` as an alternative (used one at a time). Metrics: balanced accuracy, macro-F1 and Malignant recall, never plain accuracy (always-Malignant already reaches 69%).

## 3. Quality issues found and how they were handled
* Session 2 bug: the class list was hard-coded with 10 classes and **VASC was silently dropped**. Class names are now read from `training_gt.csv`.
* `AKIEC` maps to two diagnosis_1 values (see above), so the label strategy handles it explicitly.
* Missing values: `anatom_site_general` 37% → explicit "unknown" level (missingness is informative: lesions without a site are +11 pp NV, -10 pp SCCKA). `age_approx` 0.4% → train-median imputation inside the model pipeline. `anatom_site_special` (98%) is dropped. `diagnosis_3/4` and `melanocytic` are label columns.
* **Label leaks (never inputs):** `diagnosis_2-4`, `diagnosis_confirm_type` (87% Benign when clinically confirmed vs 26% after histopathology), `concomitant_biopsy`, `melanocytic`.
* **Shortcut:** `image_manipulation = altered` is 13% Indeterminate vs 2% for unaltered images. It is not an input, and results will also be reported on unaltered images. `image_type` is perfectly balanced (no leak).
* Integrity: the image folder holds 10,480 files. `scripts/01_integrity_check.py` checks that every metadata row resolves to a file and runs `Image.verify()` on **every** image (counts of missing/unreadable files and min/median/max sizes in `outputs/image_size_summary.csv`). The loaders now **fail loudly** on missing files unless `allow_missing=True` is passed explicitly.

## 4. Preprocessing choices
* **Resolution:** shorter side resized to **256**, crop **224×224**. The images are about 600×450. `outputs/image_size_summary.csv` gives min/median/max sizes and the share of images whose shorter side is ≥ 224 and ≥ 256 (`pct_shorter_side_ge_*`), so the network input never requires upscaling. 224 is therefore pure downsampling (no upscaling artefacts) and is the input size of ImageNet-pretrained backbones. 256→224 leaves room for random crops.
* **Normalisation:** per-channel mean/std computed on the **train split only** after Resize(256)+CenterCrop(224) → `outputs/norm_stats.json`. Val and test images never influence the statistics.
* **Two views per lesion.** Implemented: **(a)** each image is an independent sample with its lesion's label (`MILK10kImageDataset`). This doubles the data and is simple, and predictions are averaged per lesion for evaluation (`aggregate_predictions`). Alternatives: **(b)** one image type only (e.g. dermoscopic), which is simpler and matches dermoscopy-only clinics but discards half the information; **(c)** both views per lesion in one sample (`LesionDataset`, already written for A3.6), which is the natural input for a two-branch/late-fusion model in a later milestone. **Leakage risk if evaluated per image:** each lesion counts twice with correlated errors, and if images (not lesions) were split, one view in train makes the other view in test a recognisable near-duplicate. A3.1 shows a sibling oracle reaching 0.85 balanced accuracy that way.
* **Augmentations** (train only, `milk10k_pipeline/transforms.py`, table in `reports/augmentation_table.md`): RandomResizedCrop (scale 0.75-1), horizontal/vertical flips, random 90° rotations (orientation-free, lossless), ColorJitter brightness/contrast 0.1, saturation 0.05, **hue 0**. The A3.5 audit shows that hue=0.02 already moves lesion hue by 3.2 deg (almost the whole 3.45 deg Benign-Malignant gap) and hue>=0.05 exceeds it, and colour is diagnostic; brightness 0.1 moves V by 7.7, below the 10-unit class gap and far below the within-class spread (std about 30). `eval_transform` is deterministic (asserted).

## 5. Why accuracy here will not mean accuracy in the clinic
MILK10k does not reflect real-world prevalence. 96% of its lesions were confirmed by histopathology, i.e. they were **biopsied**, and a lesion is only biopsied when a clinician already suspects it. The dataset is therefore the *end* of a clinical filter: 69% of lesions are malignant and BCC alone is 48%. In a primary-care or screening population the vast majority of lesions are benign nevi and seborrhoeic keratoses, and melanoma prevalence is a few per thousand. Two consequences follow. First, any accuracy, precision or PPV measured on our test split is inflated by the prevalence. A trivial "always Malignant" rule scores 69% accuracy here and would mean biopsying everyone in a clinic. Predictive values must be recomputed for the target prevalence, and we report prevalence-independent metrics (per-class recall/sensitivity and specificity, balanced accuracy, ROC curves). Second, the benign lesions in MILK10k are not *typical* benign lesions: they are the suspicious-looking ones that were cut out. The model learns to separate "suspicious benign" from malignant, which is a harder and different task than the one in a clinic, so its operating threshold will need recalibration on a representative sample before any use.

## 6. What could still go wrong
**Residual leakage:** splits are grouped by lesion, but we have no *patient* id. Several lesions of the same patient (same skin, camera and day) can fall in different splits, and images from the same contributor share colour and compression fingerprints (the A2.2 file-size check found no shortcut, AUC about 0.5, but colour calibration per centre was not tested). Near-duplicate photos across lesions were not hunted exhaustively. **Biases:** biopsy enrichment (Section 5), few lesions of darker skin types, few rare classes (MAL_OTH has 9 lesions, so its metrics are anecdotal), age and site distributions tied to the contributing centres, and the "altered" images. **For a clinician:** the model is a second reader trained on lesions that were already suspicious enough to biopsy. Its probabilities are not calibrated to a normal clinic population, it has not been validated on other cameras, phones or skin types, it cannot see the patient's history or the evolution of the lesion, and a "benign" output must never override clinical concern.
